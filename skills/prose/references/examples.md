# Examples

These examples demonstrate decisions, not reusable templates. Do not copy their rhythm into every project.

## 1. Local AI product hero

### Weak

> Your intelligent workspace for a more productive future
>
> Harness cutting-edge AI to unlock your knowledge, streamline meetings, and focus on what matters most.

### Better

> Search your files and meeting notes with AI that runs on your computer
>
> Record, transcribe, summarize, and ask questions without uploading your documents or audio.

Why it works: it names the tasks, mechanism, and privacy boundary. It does not ask the reader to believe an emotional claim.

## 2. Consultant positioning

### Weak

> Transform your business with strategic AI expertise
>
> I empower ambitious companies to navigate the complex AI landscape and unlock sustainable growth.

### Better

> I design and implement AI systems for small and mid-sized companies
>
> Projects include private on-device assistants, document workflows, cloud inference, and the infrastructure needed to run them.

Why it works: it states who, what, and examples of the work. The reader can decide whether the service fits.

## 3. Therapist product section

### Weak

> Stay present. Work smarter. Deliver better care.
>
> Our seamless platform transforms every session into actionable insights.

### Better

> Keep your attention on the client, not the notebook
>
> Voicenotes records and transcribes the session locally, then turns it into a draft note you can review after the appointment.

Why it works: it describes the situation and product behavior. It avoids implying clinical outcomes that the product cannot prove.

## 4. Feature description

### Weak

> Effortless document intelligence
>
> Unlock powerful insights from all your content in seconds.

### Better

> Ask a question across several documents
>
> Voicenotes searches the files you added and shows which passages support the answer.

Why it works: the copy tells the user what happens and includes the trust mechanism.

## 5. Privacy statement

### Weak

> Privacy you can trust
>
> Your data is always protected by our secure, privacy-first architecture.

### Better

> Your files stay on your computer
>
> Document indexing and model inference run locally. Voicenotes does not need to upload the file contents to answer a question.

Why it works: the claim is inspectable. It avoids claiming absolute security.

## 6. Pricing CTA

### Weak

> Ready to revolutionize your workflow?
>
> Start your journey today.

### Better

> Try Voicenotes on your own files
>
> Download the desktop app.

Why it works: it names the next action and removes the staged question.

## 7. Preserve a good original

Original:

> I work directly with the team that owns the problem. I do not hand the project to a junior consultant after the first call.

Do not rewrite this into:

> Benefit from senior-level expertise and personalized attention throughout every stage of your engagement.

The original is direct, specific, and commercially relevant. Keep it.

## 8. Technical article opening

### Weak

> In today's rapidly changing technology landscape, observability has become more important than ever. Let's take a closer look at how our team transformed its alerting strategy.

### Better

> Our alerting system fired sixteen times during one incident. Fourteen alerts described the same database timeout.

Why it works: the opening supplies a concrete result and leaves one useful question unresolved: why did one failure produce sixteen pages?

## 9. Meta-signposting

### Weak

> This is where the problem gets interesting. The retry queue had no upper bound.

### Better

> The retry queue had no upper bound.

Why it works: the deleted sentence contained no information.

## 10. Honest success narrative

Source fact:

> The migration completed on schedule because the team rehearsed it twice in staging.

Do not rewrite this as:

> The migration nearly failed, but the team turned a high-stakes crisis into a success.

Write:

> The team rehearsed the migration twice in staging. The production run completed on schedule, using the same sequence and rollback checks.

Why it works: it explains the success without inventing adversity.

## 11. Article closing

### Weak

> In conclusion, effective alerting requires teams to balance visibility, reliability, and speed.

### Better

> We now page on service impact and keep duplicate dependency alerts in the dashboard, where they still help diagnosis without waking anyone up.

Why it works: the ending states the resulting operating decision instead of summarizing the article.
