## 1. Page-level information order

A visitor should be able to answer these questions without decoding slogans:

1. What is this?
2. Is it meant for me?
3. What can I do with it?
4. Why is it different from my current method?
5. Why should I believe the claims?
6. What does it cost or what happens next?

Do not force this exact order when the market already understands the category. Do not hide category information for suspense.

## 2. Hero sections

A hero normally needs:

- a headline that identifies the useful result or product behavior;
- a supporting line that adds mechanism, audience, or constraint;
- a CTA that names the next action;
- optional proof that is real and immediately relevant.

The headline and supporting line must add different information.

Weak:

> Work smarter with private AI
>
> A smarter, safer way to transform your everyday work.

Stronger:

> Record and search your meetings without sending them to the cloud
>
> Voicenotes records, transcribes, and summarizes on your Mac or Windows PC.

Do not force a slogan. A direct sentence is acceptable.

## 3. Feature sections

Group features by user task, workflow stage, or decision criterion. Do not default to three equal cards.

For each feature, prefer this order:

1. Name the task in the user's language.
2. State what the product does.
3. Add the mechanism or consequence only when it clarifies the value.

Weak:

> Effortless knowledge management
>
> Unlock the power of your information with intelligent organization.

Stronger:

> Ask questions across your files
>
> Add PDFs, notes, and documents, then search their contents from one place.

## 4. How-it-works sections

Include a process section only when the process reduces doubt. Do not add one because landing pages usually have one.

Use real steps with real nouns and verbs. Avoid steps such as Connect, Transform, Thrive.

Better:

1. Choose the folder or file.
2. Voicenotes indexes it on your computer.
3. Ask a question and open the cited source.

## 5. Trust and proof

Rank proof from strongest to weakest:

1. verifiable result with method and timeframe;
2. customer quote with name and context;
3. screenshot or product demonstration;
4. technical mechanism;
5. founder experience relevant to the claim;
6. unsupported adjective.

Do not disguise mechanism as proof when the buyer needs independent evidence. Do not invent placeholders that look real.

Privacy and security claims must say what data moves where. Avoid "secure by design" unless the design is then described.

## 6. About pages

Do not write a company myth. State:

- why the company was started;
- what problem or constraint led to the product;
- what the team believes that changes product decisions;
- relevant experience;
- what remains unresolved or intentionally limited, when useful.

Avoid origin stories that use destiny, obsession, journey, mission, passion, or changing the world without specific evidence.

## 7. Consulting and service pages

Decision-makers need scope, judgment, and commercial clarity.

State:

- the problems accepted;
- the form of engagement;
- what the consultant personally does;
- what the client must provide;
- typical outputs;
- price or pricing basis when available;
- boundaries and exclusions.

Do not present a solo consultant as a vague "we" unless there is a real team. Avoid agency language that hides who performs the work.

## 8. Calls to action

Name the action and, when useful, the immediate consequence.

Prefer:

- Book a 30-minute call
- Download the Windows app
- See how local transcription works
- Send the project brief
- Start the desktop trial

Avoid:

- Get started
- Learn more
- Explore possibilities
- Begin your journey
- Unlock access

A generic CTA can be retained when the interface context already makes the action obvious, but a specific CTA is the default.

## 9. Section deletion rules

Delete a section when:

- it repeats an earlier claim;
- it exists only to fit a common landing-page template;
- the evidence is too weak for the heading;
- all its text could belong to a competitor;
- the visual design, not the content, is doing all the work;
- it introduces objections the buyer is unlikely to have;
- it increases page length without changing a decision.
