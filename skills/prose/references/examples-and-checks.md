# Examples and behavioural checks

These examples illustrate decisions. They are not templates to reproduce in every artifact. The personal examples use facts supplied in the skill-creation conversation. Do not attribute those facts to another author.

## Preserve emotional strength

Source: "I love what I do. I don't really consider it work. I get paid to do what I love. In my free time I build applications and fiddle with my homelab."

Weak revision: "I feel fortunate to have developed a career aligned with my interests."

Possible revision: "I love what I do, and getting paid for something I'd spend my free time doing anyway means a lot to me. Even outside work, I'm building applications or fiddling with my homelab."

The weak revision reduces love to interest and removes the detail that makes the feeling understandable. The possible revision keeps the strength of the statement without inventing a childhood origin story or heroic work habits.

## An example must support a point

Weak: "I welcome feedback. I enjoy helping people. At Leap, developers waited a long time for builds."

If the paragraph is about accepting feedback, there is no supplied example of feedback changing a decision. Do not invent one. If the paragraph is about platform experience, select that point and explain its relevance.

Possible revision, given the supplied production experience and target role: "I've improved an existing AWS platform while also helping keep it running. That experience would be useful as the team takes responsibility for OMRON Connect, because changes need to work for the engineers using the platform and the people relying on the service."

This is a draft interpretation of fit. It does not claim personal healthcare experience. It omits build timing because that particular detail is unnecessary to this argument.

## Explain the actual action

Source facts: The team rebuilt an image after it had already passed tests. Test environments also took too long to prepare. The changes reduced pipeline time.

Weak: "Looking into them led us to repeated work in the pipelines and test setup that was taking longer than it needed to."

Possible revision: "We found that the pipeline built the same code twice and spent too long preparing the test environment. We removed the extra build and shortened the setup, so developers got their test results sooner."

The revision names the actors and actions. It does not invent happier customers, a revenue increase or an improvement in release safety.

## Keep the recipient's role straight

Context: A recruiter receives an application to an engineering team.

Weak: "If we work together, I want you to challenge my technical decisions."

Possible revision: "I enjoy working with engineers who question my ideas, because they can spot things I've missed."

Keep this sentence only if it advances the paragraph's point and reflects supplied evidence. A correct pronoun does not make a random personality statement relevant.

## Preserve uncertainty and register

Source: "The restart restored service. We suspect connection exhaustion, but haven't confirmed it."

Weak revision: "We identified the root cause and quickly restored a reliable service."

Possible revision: "Service recovered after the restart. We suspect the application ran out of connections, but we're still checking."

The rewrite is more conversational while preserving the difference between recovery and diagnosis. No enthusiasm, personal anecdote or rhetorical ending is needed.

## Avoid an automatic rewrite

Source: "Could you send the invoice again? The attachment won't open."

If asked to humanize it, retain it unless the author supplies a specific tone change. It already gives a clear request and a reason. Do not replace it with an elaborate expression of gratitude or a generic hope that the recipient is well.

## Review rubric

Use pass, needs revision or not applicable. This is an editorial rubric, not an AI probability or a scientific score.

| Dimension | Observable check |
|---|---|
| Fidelity | Every factual claim is supported. Attribution, uncertainty and emotional intensity survive. |
| Purpose | The intended reader can identify what the author wants them to understand or do. |
| Coherence | Each paragraph develops a point, and examples visibly support that point. |
| Voice | Distinctive author language survives. No invented habits, slang or feelings appear. |
| Clarity | References resolve, actions are identifiable and causal steps can be paraphrased. |
| Audience | Pronouns, tone and assumed knowledge fit the actual recipient. |
| Economy | A detail earns its space. Necessary explanations have not been cut to meet a stylistic preference. |
| Delivery | If a file or website edit was requested, that artifact contains the revision. |

For future behavioural evaluation, give a fresh reviewer the skill and a realistic request without showing these suggested rewrites first. Useful cases include a passionate personal statement, an uncertain technical update, a recruiter-facing letter with an irrelevant anecdote, and an already-clear email. Inspect outcomes against the rubric instead of demanding a specific phrase.

A passing skill-format validator only checks packaging. It does not validate editorial judgment. Keep evaluation claims scoped to the cases actually exercised.
