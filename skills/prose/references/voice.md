# The voice pass

Make the reader understand what this particular person means, why they care, and why they are saying it here. Work on the argument and paragraph relationships before polishing sentences.

## Establish the voice and purpose

Use the current request, supplied writing, and accepted corrections to identify:

- The author, the actual recipient, their relationship, and the decision or understanding the text should support.
- Facts and experience boundaries that must survive, including uncertainty, attribution and technical meaning.
- The author's expressed feelings, intensity, characteristic words, and punctuation preferences.
- The format and length constraints. A request to humanize does not automatically mean shorten, make casual, or add a story.

Treat the author's own words and corrections as stronger voice evidence than earlier assistant drafts, even if a draft was provisionally accepted. Feedback from a detector is a fallible hypothesis about style. It is not evidence of the author's voice.

If the material is sufficient, proceed. Ask only when a missing fact or choice would materially change the message. Do not invent experience or motivation to fill a gap. For a small edit, use the surrounding paragraph as context without expanding into an unrequested full rewrite.

## Repair the thought before the wording

For a full piece, privately summarize its point in one sentence. Give each paragraph a purpose that advances that point. A paragraph should develop one idea through explanation, evidence, consequence or reflection. A list of individually true personality traits is not a developed paragraph.

Read each sentence after the previous one. Can the reader explain why this sentence comes next? Identify the actual relationship: cause, example, qualification, sequence, contrast or consequence. Reorder ideas or supply a missing explanation when necessary. Adding "also" or "that curiosity" cannot repair an unrelated example.

Introduce unfamiliar details through what the reader already knows. Resolve vague references such as "them," "those problems," "this approach" and "that work." Use stable names when synonyms would make the reader wonder whether the subject changed.

Choose detail by its contribution to the purpose. An accurate anecdote can still waste space. Either explain what it demonstrates and why the reader needs it, or omit it. Do not make the reader reconstruct the case for relevance.

Check informational value as well as coherence. In an application, ask what a sentence reveals about this person that the reader would not already assume from the job title or find in the CV. Generic duties are not evidence of fit merely because they match the vacancy. Keep details that reveal motivation, judgment, a particular way of working or a meaningful contribution. They need not be unique to this person, but they must add understanding. Remove empty duties rather than inventing a distinctive story to justify them.

## Preserve the person

Keep explicit feeling at the strength the author gave it. "I love doing this" must not quietly become "I have an interest in this." A stated feeling is source material, not an unsupported claim that needs replacing with a metric.

Keep distinctive phrases when they are clear. Lightly repair spelling or grammar without upgrading ordinary language into professional-sounding abstractions. Let supporting details deepen the feeling rather than forcing every emotional sentence to justify itself.

Do not invent late nights, childhood interests, setbacks, customer reactions, habits, dialogue or personal stakes. Distinguish what happened from what the author hopes to contribute. Preserve team credit and uncertainty.

Address the reader appropriately. A recruiter can receive a personal letter without becoming the hypothetical future teammate in every use of "you" or "we." Warmth does not require pretending familiarity or adding humour.

## Make the language easy to follow

Prefer identifiable people or systems doing concrete things. Replace noun piles and vague process language with the action they stand for. Keep technical terms when they are the clearest words for this audience. Explain an unfamiliar term when the reader needs it.

Use contractions if they fit the author and setting. Let sentence length follow the thought: a longer sentence may be needed to connect a reason to an action. Split a sentence when its dependencies are hard to hold in mind, not because it exceeded a universal word count.

Allow natural repetition of an important word, especially when it preserves continuity. Remove repetition that merely restates a claim. Avoid engineered alternation of long and short sentences, forced fragments, decorative metaphors, stock praise, automatic three-part lists and tidy moral lessons after every paragraph. Avoid staged "X, not Y" contrasts, ceremonial openings and pompous vocabulary, whatever the author's register.

Keep enough explanation for the reader to follow the reasoning. Do not compress the author's point into slogans or an inventory of traits.

Do not add typos, slang, filler, fake hesitation or irrelevant personal details to make writing appear human. Do not remove a useful hedge or passive construction solely because a checker dislikes it.

## Owner voice defaults

When the environment provides the owner's personal voice defaults (agentkit records them in `~/.config/agentkit/AGENTS.local.md`), apply them when writing in that author's voice. They are stated preferences, not rules about how humans write. Never transfer one author's preferences or biographical details to another author.

## Adapt to the artifact

For a cover letter, develop the connection between motivation, relevant experience and the employer's needs. Select one or two useful examples and interpret them instead of reproducing CV bullets or explaining the obvious. Keep the recruiter and engineering team distinct. Usually stay within one page, but follow the actual application limit. A fixed four-paragraph formula is optional.

For an email, preserve the reason for contacting the recipient and the next action. For technical prose, keep causal steps, uncertainty and exact technical distinctions. For personal prose, protect the emotional centre and the author's characteristic expression. For product copy, explain what it does for the reader without attributing an invented personal history to the brand.

## Review and deliver

Review the finished passage as continuous prose, including its neighbours. Check:

1. Can the intended reader paraphrase the point after one reading?
2. Does each example have an evident purpose and a supported connection to the claim?
3. Does the rewrite preserve the facts, the degree of certainty and the author's emotional intensity?
4. Could the author comfortably say this, without having to decode it first?
5. Does the ending belong to this piece, and does the text accomplish its practical purpose?

Resolve any failure before delivery. Read aloud if practical, or review the cadence silently. Do not claim a literal read-aloud test or independent review unless it happened. Stop when the text works, rather than rewriting merely to produce another version.

Return one committed version unless alternatives are requested. Keep any explanation brief and outside the artifact. When authorized to edit a website or document, update its actual source and relevant saved state, then verify the delivered text. A draft in chat is not a completed website edit. Do not overwrite unrelated personal edits.

When both passes run (the default for this skill), apply the evidence checks in context and do the final voice-and-coherence review after linting. A score cannot overrule a user's stated feeling, necessary connective sentence, ordinary sign-off or precise technical word. Do not optimize toward detector probabilities, sentence-length averages, function-word quotas or "burstiness."
